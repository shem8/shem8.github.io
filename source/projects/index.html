Side Projects

20+ side projects in two years, built on nights and weekends next to a full-time job, mostly with AI agents. Building is the easy part now. Operating, distribution and attention are the hard parts. Money is a bonus, not the plan.

I talk about this in two of my talks: “I Have a Full-Time Job. My Side Projects Don’t” and “A Platform Team of One, With AI”.

Products

Kickoff

A fan’s home for World Cup 2026 resale-ticket data, tracking resale prices for every match. It’s a Chrome extension: fans browse FIFA’s resale site as usual and each browser sends back what it sees. There is no central scraper, so nothing gets blocked.

  • 104 matches covered, 8.1M seat changes tracked, $0 spent on scraping.
  • Market-update posts in r/WorldCup2026Tickets reached 455K views across three posts and brought almost 1,000 users.
  • Covered by WIRED and The Times.

Cherish Motion

Turns old family photos into moving-memory videos: colorized and brought to life. The landing page came first, with the product behind it. AI-generated ads found a market I would never have guessed: Spanish speakers. It got real orders and real revenue, and a TikTok ad took off one Friday night and the site went down while I slept.

It runs on a custom back office: one screen per order (select images, edit, animate, create video), usable from a phone.

Park Autopilot

A theme park planning app for families, with real-time “what to do next” recommendations and a $4.99 day pass. It was built in about 10 hours by a team of AI agents (CEO, PM, devs, QA, marketer), supervised and approved by me through MissionControl. An agent drafts the blog posts every week and opens a PR for review.

Distribution was the real experiment, at about $1,000 total:

  • Reddit (banned).
  • 60 Disney bloggers and influencers (1 free trial, 0 paying).
  • 45+ SEO blog posts, Pinterest and Show HN.
  • Offline ads in Orlando: airport screens were rejected; taxi back-seat tablets cost $500 for 20,000 views.

Sports Copilot

A phone placed behind the kicker detects each kick and where it hit the goal, with an Accuracy Challenge scored by zone. The models worked on paper but failed on a real pitch, so I built my own goal-labeling studio and filmed real kicks with real kids to make it work.

HelpsHub

Personal AI experts on WhatsApp for fitness, nutrition and finance, with agents like Ariel (nutrition), Ben (training coach) and Mia (travel agent). Built with a Twilio number, Meta’s WhatsApp business review, a waiting list and checkout.

The honest outcome: few users. The lesson was “I built the whole thing before anyone asked for it.”

Launch Gate

A $19 launch-readiness kit, fully automated, built entirely by my agent Instinct during a 30-hour offline challenge on Yom Kippur. The brief: think of 50 ideas, pick the 3 most likely to make money within 24 hours, and start from a $10 budget.

The same run produced CSV Preflight ($19, Shopify CSV repair) and Resume Gap Brief ($12, resume vs. job description).

The outcome: ~190 outreach emails, ~330 DEV comments, 5 articles, and developers credited Launch Gate publicly. No confirmed sales.

OnePercent

A professional growth mentor, one percent at a time. First built by hand in 2020, then rebuilt with AI agents in 2026. The same basic problems remained: SEO, hosting, A/B tests.

Built for myself

Small, personal, used for real. I’m the first user.

  • Personal trainer: a daily workout app with streaks and academy points, built for one real user, my son.
  • Real Madrid seats: a seat-finding tool for four adjacent seats. It got the tickets.
  • US road trip helpers (summer 2026): an illustrated, printable family trip booklet, a US hotels monitor and a US content map.
  • Telegram to Google Drive: a helper bot.
  • Electricity-plan calculator
  • Leveraged property investment calculator: models equity recycling and compounding leverage.
  • Project dashboard
  • Roblox parkour-building game: built in Roblox Studio with Luau, entirely from the Command Bar.

Agent infrastructure

  • Instinct: my personal agent, which I talk to over WhatsApp. It does research and even whole products (Launch Gate). Built on cc-bridge.
  • MissionControl: a board where I watch my agent team and review and approve their work, by channel.
  • Scheduled agent jobs: recurring content, reports and checks. They open drafts or PRs and never ship on their own.
  • An old Mac at home: always on; agents, jobs and bots run there.

Stack

  • Claude Code (~$100/mo): almost everything, from code and ops to copy and back office
  • Cloudflare ($5/mo): workers, database, storage, queues, cron
  • Polar ($0 + fee per sale): payments, no company setup
  • Resend: email
  • Replicate (pay per run): video and image models
