Side Projects

I’ve been building things on the side for as long as I’ve been writing code. It started with hacking stuff together in high school, and I never really stopped. Whatever my day job was, there was always another idea I couldn’t get out of my head, and another late night when I finally sat down to build it.

Some of these projects found real users, some were made just for me and my family, and plenty never went anywhere. I love them all anyway. Side projects are where I get to try things I’d never try at work, learn from my own mistakes, and own everything from the first line of code to the first customer. I even wrote a whole guide about it.

It’s the best way I know to keep learning, and it’s still the most fun I have with a computer. There’s always another one on the way.

Kickoff

A fan’s home for World Cup 2026 resale-ticket data. A Chrome extension: fans browse FIFA’s resale site as usual and each browser sends back what it sees, so there is no central scraper to block. It covered 104 matches and tracked 8.1M seat changes for $0 in scraping costs, and market-update posts on Reddit reached 455K views and brought almost 1,000 users. Covered by WIRED and The Times.

Cherish Motion

Turns old family photos into moving-memory videos: colorized and brought to life. The landing page came first, with the product behind it, and AI-generated ads found a market I would never have guessed: Spanish speakers. It got real orders and real revenue, and runs on a custom back office usable from a phone.

Park Autopilot

A theme park planning app for families, with real-time “what to do next” recommendations and a $4.99 day pass. Built in about 10 hours by a team of AI agents that I supervise through MissionControl. Distribution was the real experiment, at about $1,000 total: Reddit (banned), 60 Disney bloggers (0 paying), 45+ SEO posts, and taxi tablets in Orlando.

Sports Copilot

A phone placed behind the kicker detects each kick and where it hit the goal, with an Accuracy Challenge scored by zone. The models worked on paper but failed on a real pitch, so I built my own goal-labeling studio and filmed real kicks to make it work.
